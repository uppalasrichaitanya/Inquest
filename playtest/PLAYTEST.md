# Watching a new player

One person, about 20 minutes, on their own phone if they have one. They have never seen Inquest. You watch; you don't help.

## Before
- Clear the game's storage on their device, or use a private window, so the warm-up opens by itself.
- Have this page open, and a notes sheet with the columns below. Note the time at each moment you write down.
- Don't play the case yourself first in front of them. Don't mention the warm-up, the notebook or the Hint button.

## What to say
At the start, word for word:
> "This is a small daily puzzle game. I'd like to watch you try it for the first time. There's no wrong way to play, and I'm testing the game, not you. Please think out loud: say what you're looking at and what you expect to happen. I won't help, but I'll answer anything once you're done."

If they go quiet for more than 20 seconds: "What are you thinking right now?"
If they ask you something: "What would you try?" (Answer it properly at the end.)
If they're stuck for more than three minutes and want to stop, let them. Note where.

## What not to say
- Don't explain the rules, the notebook, "next to", or what a mark means.
- Don't point at anything, reach for the screen, or say "nearly", "warmer" or "not that one".
- Don't say "it's easy" or "most people get this". Don't apologise for the game.
- Don't react when they get something wrong. A wrong accusation is data.

## What to note
| Moment | What to write down |
|---|---|
| First 10 seconds | What they look at and what they tap first. Do they read the three rules, or press "Got it" straight away? |
| Hesitation | Every pause over about 5 seconds: where they were and what they said. |
| The notebook | Their first mark: a cross or a circle, on purpose or by accident? Do they use Undo? Do they notice the faint marks? |
| The statements | Which one they read first. The moment they stop reading statements in full and start skimming or guessing. |
| "Next to" | Do they use the plan? Any clue about rooms or carriages "next to" each other they misread? Quote what they say. |
| Hint | Do they find the Hint button? When, and why: stuck, or curious? Does "Spell it out" help? |
| Accusing | When they first try "Name the killer", and whether it was early. Wrong accusations, and what they say after one. |
| The finish | Do they read the sentence? Open "How it happened"? Press Share, and where would they send it? |
| Words they use | Their names for things: "grid", "board", "clues", "rooms". These are better labels than ours. |

## Afterwards (2 minutes)
Ask, without leading:
1. "What was the game asking you to do?"
2. "Was there a moment you felt lost? Where?"
3. "Which statement was the hardest to understand?" (Ask them to find it again and read it out.)
4. "Would you play tomorrow's?"

## The next day
Don't remind them. Note whether they come back on their own, at what time, and whether they finished. If they don't come back by the evening, send one line, "Did you try today's?", and note the answer word for word.

Keep the notes in `playtest/<date>-<initials>.md`.
